## Ordered material findings

### R1-01: Pagehide settlement is ordered after an existing save that can erase the old-effect interval

Priority: high. Design lines 75-77, 107 and 178 require old-effect absence before activation, but the pagehide paragraph permits a best-effort activation without specifying how it precedes the existing finalizers or preserves the hidden baseline. The generic boundary requirement is correct; the named integration is incomplete.

`rewards.js:100-105,638` invokes finalizers in registration order while ownership remains live, then revokes ownership. Core registers its wallet finalizer at `core.js:99`; Ops registers at `ops.js:1817`, before extension registration at 3047. Ops first sets `paused=true`, then calls `saveAcknowledged`, which writes `lastSeen=Date.now()` unless an away checkpoint already exists at `ops.js:210-215`. A Vault finalizer registered during its extension init therefore runs after both the pause and the timestamp replacement. Existing `simulateAway` refuses work while paused at `ops.js:1488-1489`; `welcomeBack` computes absence from the replaced `lastSeen` at 1711-1715.

Counterexample: reserve Game Speed at G=1, hide at t=2, then depart at t=8 before local expiry. The ordinary hidden save establishes t=2. The current Ops pagehide finalizer can replace it with t=8 before Vault sees it. A later Vault boundary now sees no six-second backlog. Activating at t=8 loses that ordinary work; retaining t=2 while saving the new active factor would instead let the next load apply the new factor retroactively. The existing 30/60-second welcome thresholds are also unsuitable as a transition settlement rule for this short interval. A pending interrupted-away checkpoint needs its existing cursor/identity, not a fresh interval.

Smallest correction: make one explicit departure/hidden transition order part of the design and prospective Rewards/Ops lease. Preserve the old interval start before any ordinary finalizer can advance it; finish eligible old-factor work and acknowledge its boundary before activating, or leave the reservation inactive and preserve that boundary for fresh recovery. Specify which finalizer owns `lastSeen`, how already-settled prefixes are represented, and how failure prevents a later ordinary finalizer from overwriting them. No second simulator or alternate writer is needed. Add the exact hidden-at-2/pagehide-at-8 sequence, hidden beyond expiry, preexisting checkpoint and failure at each acknowledgement to later tests. Native bfcache must still leave the old document inert.

### R1-02: Stopping progression before settlement makes the proposed coordinator unable to perform the work it promises

Priority: high. Design purchase step 2 at line 68 says to stop progression, then settle through `vaultBoundary`. Line 107 requires that boundary to finish saved/hidden work and active timer work. The new coordinator at 178 enters Rewards' transaction machinery. Both a paused Ops loop and `writing=true` make `progressing()` false at `ops.js:19-21`.

This affects more than calling `tick`: `simulateAway`, `press`, challenge continuations and ordinary wallet add/earn/spend are progression-guarded. Sources: `ops.js:550-558,970-972,1488-1489,2998-2999`; `core.js:119-121,132-136,356`; `rewards.js:130-132,275-280`. The design recognizes guard-before-writing for candidate validation, but does not resolve the same conflict for productive boundary work.

Counterexample: an expiring reservation has old-factor hidden work that should yield letters and a first-clear reward. If the coordinator stops/enters its transaction first, that work exits. If the implementation instead clears the pause to force settlement, a nested reward failure can take the hold while the outer Vault operation continues unless the continuation is explicitly specified. A new `vaultCommit` cannot repair work never performed.

Smallest correction: give the coordinator a precise prepare/settle/commit sequence. Under the already-held writer lock, settle old-factor work before entering the nonprogressing commit section, immediately honor any enclosing Rewards hold, retain a single Vault intent only if needed, then re-read state/revalidate the quote, acknowledge the current ordinary wallet baseline, and stage the Vault transition. Describe how Rewards recovery resumes settlement exactly once before the Vault intent is reconsidered. Never bypass ordinary wallet guards, mutate a stale snapshot, replay an away batch or claim a failed settlement as a successful purchase. Include a reward failure during settlement and repeated Retry in the later matrix.

### R1-03: Hold compensation omits callback ownership and mixes saved timestamps with document-local clocks

Priority: high. Design lines 109-111 correctly demand no live timestamp mutation before acknowledgement and one total compensation across retries. They also require capturing pickup clocks and stopping associated callbacks, but the prospective leases name no pickup pause/resume seam. The requirement to persist the compensated timestamp fields is ambiguous for values that do not belong in a save.

`crop.ready` and `challenge.start` are saved Date.now values. `buffs` and `productionExtra` are private document-local values at `ops.js:24,31,377-385`; buffs compare against performance.now at 387/428. Ordinary pickups have private expiry callbacks, interval work and animations at `pickups.js:18-35,68-88`. Those callbacks do not check progression. Rewards' timer wrapper checks ownership only at `rewards.js:38-45`, so holding progression does not stop them. Native golden pickups also have a real timeout or `Animation.onfinish` at `ops.js:1766-1775`, independent of the eventual `productionExtra` patch.

Counterexample: a reservation expires while the player has returned to Floor and an ordinary pickup has five seconds left. Activation persistence fails for twenty seconds. Its expiry callback can remove it during the hold; subtracting twenty seconds from `productionExtra` after Retry cannot restore the removed object or canceled animation. Broadly canceling all Rewards timers instead also cancels news/presentation callbacks, contrary to the promised clock separation. Persisting a performance.now expiry would be meaningless in a fresh document.

Smallest correction: enumerate the compensation fields by clock and lifetime. Stage only saved crop/challenge changes in the durable candidate; keep native buff/production/pickup continuation patches volatile and publish them once after the successful acknowledgement. Add a narrow, explicitly owned pickup callback/animation pause-resume contract, plus the native golden expiry path, or present a clearly different owner-approved hold policy. Do not serialize monotonic clock values or build a general scheduler. Define `lastSeen`/hidden-hold treatment so successful same-process recovery does not subsequently simulate the held interval as ordinary away work, while preserving work before the hold. Specify Date.now elapsed compensation separately from monotonic Undo duration. Later failure tests must assert unchanged live state through repeated failures, preserved remaining lifetime on success, no duplicated ordinary income, and unchanged news/calendar behavior.

### R1-04: A same-build return without lg02 can bypass the permanent-ownership/reset contract

Priority: high for enabling purchases. Design lines 184/193 acknowledges unguarded disabled/stale builds, but classifies the boundary as a compatibility limitation and test rather than providing a safe supported transition or an explicit blocked-use policy.

`rewards.js:4-5` is enabled solely by `lg01a`. `economy.js:31-32` makes the disabled path an unrestricted writer. A same-build `?lg01a=1` page without `lg02` still uses the shared save, while the proposed Vault module does nothing. Its ordinary full reset at `ops.js:2967` clears `prem`, claims and ownership. A fully unflagged page can also write without the lock and drops wallet metadata through `economy.js:75`'s ordinary save shape. These routes do not require a stale service worker or a malicious save edit.

Counterexample: purchase an item under both flags, close, then open an existing bookmark without lg02 and choose Reset. The owner-selected Vault reset policy and purchase guard never run. Merely preserving unknown `prem.vault` fields during ordinary load does not protect the timeline.

The enabled in-game edition links already preserve `location.search` in both shells. Do not repair `style-swap.js` unnecessarily: it is not loaded when Rewards is enabled. The unresolved case is direct/current-disabled navigation and mixed loaded sources, not those existing guarded links.

Smallest correction: choose and explicitly lease a current-build compatibility gate that recognizes a Vault-bearing save before gameplay/wallet writers/reset, without activating purchases or changing the no-Vault default path. Alternatively require a concrete isolated test-save/origin boundary and keep general-save purchase enablement blocked until that gate exists. Old code already loaded cannot be made cooperative by a new module; retain that stale-build limitation honestly and define the supported cache/update boundary. No public activation is authorized here. Test same-build missing flags separately from old/noncooperating documents.

### R1-05: The contract incorrectly says individual taps receive Game Speed through dt

Priority: medium. Design catalogue line 31 and clock line 83 combine manual taps and Hold as receiving Game Speed. `ops.js:2850-2858` calls `press(S.sel,false)` once for a physical tap/key press. `press` adds a letter without reading dt or speed at 550-558. Hold accumulates `holdRate * dt` at 3012, so it does receive G. Automatic typists receive G through elapsed dt and the proposed T through `typistSpeed`.

Counterexample: ten separate taps at G=1 versus G=2 still produce ten base presses, excluding existing Golden Keys. A held interval produces twice the held work at G=2. Multiplying individual taps to match the current sentence would introduce a new yield/stat/RNG mechanic.

Smallest correction: distinguish physical taps, held typing and automatic typists in catalogue/copy/tests. Keep individual tap yield unchanged; Hold gets G only; automatic typists get G*T. If extra yield per tap is intended, return it as a separate owner proposal rather than implying it follows from the accepted clock. Retain both x2 maxima.

### R1-06: Fourth-seat integration leaves a three-Muse award/goal semantic conflict

Priority: medium. Design line 119 says to preserve the accepted three-Muse predicate and line 182 forbids award predicate changes. The source implements that old three-seat world using `S.muses.seated.slice(0,3)` in both `mu3` at `ops.js:1267` and goal facts at 1319. With a fourth usable seat those expressions count positions, not all active Muses.

Counterexample: seats 1, 2 and 4 occupied, seat 3 empty. Three valid Muses are active through the proposed effective-capacity provider, but the unchanged award and goal report two. This is not a request to require four Muses or change the reward.

Smallest correction: explicitly resolve how the existing three-Muse requirement counts the new seat and include the corresponding narrow predicate/facts lease if preserving the player-facing meaning. Keep threshold, ID, once-only receipt and reward unchanged. Test every three-of-four combination and duplicate swaps. Also correct the phrase "authored sold counts" at line 119: `soldCount()` at `ops.js:403-412` excludes kids/library titles but includes generated pitches; `authoredSold()` is a different function. Preserve the existing soldCount gate rather than making the paid seat stricter by accident.
